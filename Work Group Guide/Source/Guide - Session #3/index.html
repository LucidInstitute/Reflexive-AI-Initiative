About this guide

Thank you for contributing to the Reflexive AI research initiative. We are grateful for your time and effort.

This facilitation guide helps you run a work group about reflexive AI. Reflexivity means “to step back and view oneself from a distance to become critically aware of oneself in relation to larger contexts”. For this initiative, it means reflecting on how your use of AI is (or is not) influenced by your values, principles and norms and how this in turn influences the people around you, your organization and broader society.

The aim for each work group is to meet at least three times over a period of time to contribute to the broader Reflexive AI research initiative. The work groups are based on participatory action research, in which scientists and professionals collaboratively and iteratively address societal challenges. The methodology is outlined here.

This guide is for Session 3. It takes 3 hours and can be attended by 5–30 participants. It builds on the outcomes of Sessions 1 and 2: the pre-mortem posters, the question storming posters, the action recipes and the actions participants committed to. Where Session 2 made the framework concrete, Session 3 makes it transferable. Sub-groups capture their action recipes in enough depth that people who were not in the room can understand and use them. The group then hears a synthesis of the work done so far across work groups, reflects on the process as a whole, and decides how to continue.

Session roadmap

Session 1
  • Create connections between participants.
  • Clarify the aim of the initiative and how participants relate to it as co-researchers.
  • Create a first, broad increment of (part of) the framework.
  • Participants plan a concrete intervention they want to try within their organization before the next session.
Session 2
  • Evaluate actions taken since the previous session, as well as input from the World Cafés, and use it to iterate the framework.
  • Develop interventions, strategies and actions for the themes more deeply, captured as action recipes.
  • Participants plan a concrete intervention they want to try within their organization before the next session.
Session 3 · this guide
  • Evaluate actions taken since the previous session and use them to iterate the action recipes.
  • Audit the action recipes, and capture them in rich written, audio or video form so they can be shared and published.
  • Evaluate the process and outcomes to date, informed by a synthesis across work groups.
  • Participants plan a concrete action, building on the reflection on the process.

The above is an outline. The aim of this session may be adjusted based on group preferences.

Contributing to the Reflexive AI initiative

We designed this guide for work groups that contribute to our global research initiative. This is not required — you can run this work group within your organization in isolation. If you do contribute to the research initiative, please ensure:

  • That you coordinate with our team at reflexiveai.org so we can ensure academic rigor, pre- and post-session surveys, and informed consent by all participants. We publish outcomes and materials on GitHub.
  • That all participants are aware they are participating in a research initiative, and that the (anonymous) results from the discussions will be shared with other work groups and on GitHub.
  • That new participants who did not attend earlier sessions fill in the informed consent form before they take part.
  • That audio and video recordings (Step 7) are only made with the explicit consent of everyone who is recorded, and are handled as research data.
  • That you do not bias participants by pushing them in a certain direction (e.g. “AI is great” or “AI is terrible”).
  • That you do not modify the format unless absolutely necessary. Informed consent must always take place at the start. If you require deviations, please let us know what you changed and how.

Please contact us at info@reflexiveai.org with questions.

How to prepare

Several days before the session

  • Send a pre-workshop email that includes:
    • A note that Session 3 focuses on capturing what we have learned in a form others can use, looking ahead, and deciding together how to continue.
    • A link to the pre-workshop survey, which is helpful for participants to prepare.
    • A request to bring a laptop to write up action recipes, and a phone to make audio or video recordings if they prefer to talk or show rather than write.
    • A request for participants who were unable to attend earlier sessions to pick the theme that best fits their expertise, so they can join that group.
  • Prepare the Action Recipe Capture Template: upload it to Google Drive as a Google Doc (or use a similar online document) in a shared folder that participants can edit during the session. It is a simple document with suggested sub-headers for describing the action; the rest is up to the participants. Groups make one copy per recipe, so prepare a few copies in advance or share a “make a copy” link.
  • Prepare a shared folder or upload link where sub-groups can drop audio and video recordings. Check with the coordination team how recordings will be transcribed and anonymized.
  • Collect any new data from the World Cafés since Session 2, so sub-groups can use it to refine their action recipes.

Materials

  • Materials from Sessions 1 and 2:
    • The filled-in pre-mortem posters for each theme, including stickies.
    • The filled-in question storming posters for each theme.
    • The filled-in action recipes, including stickies with feedback.
    • The filled-in poster for the definition of human-centered AI.
    • Both filled-in 15% Solutions posters, with the actions participants chose after Sessions 1 and 2.
    • The study roadmap and the Purpose-to-Practice Worksheet.
    • The helpful resources poster and the give & get help poster.
  • Print the What, So What, Now What poster on A0/A1 size (1 total), to capture the process debrief in Step 9.
  • Print a fresh 15% Solutions poster (1 total) for the actions planned in Step 10.
  • Print fresh action recipes on A1-2 size, 3 per expected sub-group.
  • Print the Action Recipe Context sheet on A4, one per action recipe (bring plenty, as returning recipes from Session 2 need one too).
  • The Action Recipe Capture Template, made available as a link participants can copy.
  • Print the informed consent forms for new participants (bring a few spares).
  • Print summaries of any new World Café data (PDFs), at least one set per sub-group.
  • The presentation on outcomes so far from the coordination team (Step 8), and a screen or projector to show it.
  • A stack of empty flip charts or whiteboards.
  • Bring stickies, markers and pens.
  • Laptops for writing, and phones (or voice recorders) for audio and video recordings.
  • A timer is useful to track time.
  • A buzzer or tingsha bell is useful to indicate new rounds.
  • Optionally, bring snacks and drinks for a relaxed atmosphere. As this is the final session of the arc, consider something small to celebrate.

Setting up the room

  • Populate the room beforehand. Create as many stations as in Session 2.
  • Put up the pre-mortem poster, question storming poster and action recipes of each theme at its station.
  • Put up the other materials from earlier sessions: the definition of human-centered AI, both 15% Solutions posters and the Purpose-to-Practice. These act as external memory and as stigmergic traces of the previous sessions.
  • Put a stack of fresh action recipes, Action Recipe Context sheets and the link to the capture template at each station.
  • Find a few quiet corners (or adjacent rooms) where sub-groups can make audio and video recordings in Step 7.
  • Put up the What, So What, Now What poster and the fresh 15% Solutions poster next to each other in a central spot, visible to everyone and with enough space in front for the whole group to gather. They are used one after the other in Steps 9 and 10.

10 min

Step 1. Welcome and refresher

We welcome participants and use the materials from Sessions 1 and 2 as external memory. “Walking the walls” refreshes insights for returning participants and lets new participants catch up quickly, guided by someone who was there. This exercise highlights the stigmergic traces that facilitate self-organization.

Steps

  • Welcome people as they arrive. Ensure new participants fill in the informed consent form before they join the exercise.
  • Welcome everyone and introduce yourself briefly. A more thorough opening follows after this exercise. Ask participants to form pairs, self-organizing so that each new participant is paired with at least one returning participant.
  • 7 minPairs walk around the room, in whatever order works for them, to refresh the insights and materials from the previous sessions: the pre-mortem posters, the question storming posters, the action recipes and the 15% Solutions.
  • 3 minDebrief by collecting a few insights. What stands out now, looking at everything together? What has changed in how we think about it?
5 min

Step 2. Opening

We open the third work group session.

  1. Thank the participants for returning, and welcome new participants.
  2. Reiterate informed consent: participants take part in a research initiative, and the anonymous results will be shared with other work groups and on GitHub. Explain that overview pictures (not close-ups) may be taken of the activities to share on social media, and note that anyone who prefers not to be in any pictures can approach the facilitators at a suitable time. Mention that some sub-groups may make audio or video recordings later, and that these only happen with everyone’s consent.
  3. Clarify the aim of this session: to learn from the actions taken since Session 2, to capture our action recipes in a form that others can use, to look ahead to what will make this initiative succeed, and to decide together how we continue.
  4. Set expectations that this is the last session of the three-session arc, but not necessarily the end. We will spend the final part of the session deciding what comes next.

15 min

Step 3. Evaluate actions with Impromptu Networking

In action research, taking action generates the feedback that informs the next iteration. In pairs, participants evaluate the actions they took since Session 2, many of which were based on an action recipe. New participants, or participants who did not take action, ask clarifying questions and share related experiences.

Steps

  • 4 minAsk participants to form pairs. Ask: “What action did you take since the last session? What happened? What worked or did not work? What does this tell us about the action recipe it was based on?”
  • 4 minAsk participants to form new pairs. The pairs respond to the same prompt and also look for patterns across conversations. Encourage participants to take notes to capture insights, as these are useful later in the session.
  • 4 minAsk participants to form new pairs once more. The pairs respond to the same prompt and also look for patterns across conversations. Encourage participants to take notes.
  • 3 minShared debrief with the whole group. Which themes and patterns did people notice across the conversations? Which action recipes should be improved, and how?
5 min

Step 4. Return to the theme stations

The sub-groups from Session 2 reconvene at their stations. This is also a moment to re-organize.

Steps

  • Point out the stations for the themes from Session 2.
  • Give participants the opportunity to switch sub-groups if they want to. New participants pick the theme that best suits their expertise and interest.
  • If some people have a strong desire to pick up another theme, they can do so. They take an empty station and use the theme definition from the research presentation as a starting point.
  • Check that sub-groups are of a workable size, ideally 3–5 people each.

30 min

Step 5. Refine action recipes

The sub-groups refine the action recipes for their theme, based on the actions taken since Session 2 and the patterns from Step 3. If there is time left, they create more recipes where gaps remain. The pre-mortem poster, the question storming poster and the World Café data provide input.

This does not need to be group work. If an individual participant feels they have a good idea for an action recipe, they can also create one on their own. Sub-groups can also split into pairs or individuals to work on several recipes in parallel.

Participants should also clarify the context they drew from to create each action recipe, so others can judge where it applies. For this, they fill in an Action Recipe Context sheet and attach it to the recipe: what types of organizations did they have in mind (size, type), at what stage of AI adoption is this recipe important, and how did the creators’ own background shape the recipe, both as a source of insight and of blind spots? In action research, this reflexivity is part of what makes a recipe trustworthy.

Steps

  • Briefly remind participants of the action recipe template and its guiding questions. Emphasize that they can work as a group, in pairs or alone, whatever helps them produce the best recipes.
  • 30 minParticipants first refine their existing action recipes based on what they learned from the actions taken. If there is time left, they create more recipes. Participants can use AI to help them, for example to explore options or sharpen a draft, but the participants decide what goes on the template. Aim for quality over quantity. Make sure each recipe has a filled-in Action Recipe Context sheet attached.
15 min

Step 6. Shift & Audit

Participants tour the other stations with a variation on Shift & Share. This time, visitors act as playful auditors of the action recipes at each station. Their questions help validate the recipes and show the sub-group what still needs work before the recipes are captured in Step 7.

Steps

  • Explain the sequence of steps and the auditor role. Auditors are curious and critical, but kind: the aim is to make the recipes stronger, not to judge them. Suggested audit questions:
    • “How does this sufficiently address the risks you identified for this theme (i.e. in the pre-mortem)?”
    • “How generalizable is this recipe to different kinds of organizations?”
    • “How might this recipe reflect biases of those who created it?”
  • Each sub-group picks one host. This person stays at the station throughout the exercise, presents the action recipes and answers the auditors’ questions. If you run this work group with fewer than 8 participants, you can also tour the stations as a whole group.
  • 5 minThe sub-groups — except the host — move clockwise to the next station. The auditors ask their questions; the host records findings and suggestions on stickies attached to the templates.
  • 10 minRepeat two more times, or as often as time allows. Encourage auditors to build on the stickies left by previous visitors rather than repeat them.
  • collectPictures/scans of the audited action recipes, including stickies.

40 min

Step 7. Turn action recipes into concrete outcomes

Over three sessions, the sub-groups have developed a deep understanding of their action recipes. A poster only holds a fraction of it: the context, examples, caveats and reasoning mostly live in people’s heads. The aim of this step is to ensure that this understanding is sufficiently captured, so that people who were not in the room can understand and run the recipe themselves.

Participants choose how to capture each recipe. Different formats bring out different things: writing forces precision, talking surfaces stories and nuance, and showing makes the steps tangible.

  • Write — fill in the Action Recipe Capture Template together on a laptop, using the poster, the stickies and the audit findings as a starting point.
  • Audio — record step-by-step audio instructions for running the recipe, as if guiding someone who is about to do it in their own organization.
  • Video — make a short video with step-by-step instructions to run the recipe, for example by talking through the poster or demonstrating parts of it.
  • Mix — any combination of the above, e.g. a written recipe with an audio walkthrough, or a recording that is transcribed (with AI) and then edited into the template.

Steps

  • 5 minExplain the aim and the formats. Show the capture template and where to upload recordings. Each sub-group decides which recipes to capture, in which format, and divides the work. Confirm that everyone who will be recorded consents to it. Video makers can film the poster or their hands rather than faces to make anonymization easier.
  • 25 minParticipants capture their action recipes in pairs, small groups or individually. Those recording find a quiet corner. Participants can use AI to help them, for example to transcribe a recording, structure a draft or check it for gaps, but they decide what is captured. Encourage them to go beyond the poster: “Imagine someone in another organization wants to run this recipe tomorrow. What do they need to know that is not on the poster?”
  • 10 minSwap within or between sub-groups. Each pair reads, listens to or watches a recipe captured by others and asks: “Could I run this recipe based on what is captured here? What is missing or unclear?” The authors use the feedback to fill the gaps.
  • collectThe filled-in capture templates.
  • collectThe audio and video recordings, labeled with the theme and recipe title.

10 min

Step 8. Presentation on outcomes so far

Before we reflect on the process as a whole, a member of the coordination team presents the outcomes so far, including the data reported so far across all work groups. This shows participants how their contributions add up and how they are being used, and provides shared data to reflect on in the next step. Like the research presentation in Session 1, the presentation is provided by the coordination team.

Topics covered through a presentation

  • The data reported so far by the work groups, and how it is being analyzed.
  • The themes selected across the work groups, and the themes that were not selected.
  • The evaluation of the work group process, based on the WASC scale deployed after each session.
  • Reported change readiness, based on the 4-item scale deployed before each new session as part of the reflection on the actions taken.
  • An overview of the action recipes generated so far, grouped by theme.

Note: all survey results are presented anonymously and in aggregate. Leave room for a few clarifying questions, but save discussion for the next step.

40 min

Step 9. Debrief the process with What, So What, Now What

Action research reflects not only on outcomes, but also on the collaborative process that produced them. With What, So What, Now What, we look back at the three sessions as a whole, informed by our own experience and the outcomes presented in Step 8: what worked well, and what was difficult? The outcomes are captured on the What, So What, Now What poster and become input for Step 10.

Steps

  • Invite participants to form groups of 5–7, mixed across the theme sub-groups. Explain that each round builds on the previous one, and that groups put their most important stickies in the matching row of the poster at the end of each round.
  • 8 minWhat?Ask: “Looking back at our three sessions and the actions we took in between, what happened? What worked well? What was difficult? What stood out in the presentation?” Focus on observations and facts, not yet on interpretations.
  • 10 minSo What?Ask: “Why is that important? What patterns do we notice? What does this tell us about how we work together, and about the initiative?”
  • 10 minNow What?Ask: “What should we keep, change or start doing, as a work group and as an initiative?”
  • 12 minBring everyone together. Invite each group to share a highlight from each row, and look for patterns across the groups. Pay particular attention to the “Now What?” row, as it is the starting point for Step 10.
  • collectThe filled-in What, So What, Now What poster.

10 min

Step 10. Identify actions with 15% Solutions

This session concludes the three-session arc, but not necessarily the work. We use the Liberating Structure 15% Solutions to help participants pick a concrete action, building on the “Now What?” row from the process debrief. Actions can be about continuing this work group, about the initiative as a whole, or about applying the action recipes in their own organization.

Steps

  • 3 minAsk participants to silently and individually reflect on: “Based on the ‘Now What?’ from our debrief, what is one concrete action you can take? What is within your mandate and available resources?”
  • 4 minInvite people to form pairs to share their actions and coach each other to make them more concrete and doable. Capture actions on stickies, with their name and when they will do it.
  • 3 minAsk participants who feel comfortable to share their actions. Put the stickies on the 15% Solutions poster.
  • collectThe 15% Solutions poster with actions.
5 min

Step 11. Closing

Close the session by thanking everyone for their time and their contributions as co-researchers across the three sessions. If this group participates in the research initiative, reiterate that the anonymous results will be shared with the initiative, and that research updates will be available on reflexiveai.org.

We recommend going through this checklist:

  • collectFilled-in informed consent forms for every new participant.
  • collectThe filled-in action recipes and capture templates, and the audio and video recordings.
  • collectThe filled-in What, So What, Now What and 15% Solutions posters.
  • collectAll posters from Sessions 1–3. If the group continues, keep them as “external memory” for the next session.

Step 12. Share the results

When you’ve completed the session, please share the following with the research initiative if you opted to do so.

  • The filled-in capture templates for the action recipes, and clear pictures or scans of the action recipes and posters, including any stickies or content on them. Please remove or blur people’s names and company-sensitive information. These will be published on our public GitHub to benefit the initiative and other work groups.
  • The audio and video recordings, labeled with the theme and recipe title. Recordings are transcribed and anonymized before anything is published; the recordings themselves are only published with the explicit consent of everyone in them.
  • Pictures of the What, So What, Now What and 15% Solutions posters.
  • A brief review by you and any other facilitators of how the session went. What worked? What did not? Where did you observe tension or friction?
  • Scans or pictures of filled-in informed consent forms for all new participants. Unfortunately, we cannot process any data without informed consent.
  • Send the materials to info@reflexiveai.org.

Post-session activities

After the session, facilitators individually capture their experiences, observations and reflections in a personal log. As this concludes the three-session arc, also reflect on the arc as a whole: how did the group, the framework and your own understanding change across the three sessions? What would you do differently?

If the sessions contribute to the research initiative, a short follow-up survey will be sent to the participants’ email addresses.
